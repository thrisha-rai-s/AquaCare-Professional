LEARN AND PROTECT

Basic Water Awareness

Simple everyday practices can help protect household water quality.

Safe Storage

Store drinking water in clean, covered containers and avoid touching the water directly with unclean hands.

Maintain Hygiene

Clean water containers regularly and maintain hygiene around water-storage and collection areas.

Test Your Water

Arrange appropriate water-quality testing through recognised laboratories or authorised agencies.

Use Safe Sources

Prefer approved drinking-water sources and follow public-health advisories issued by local authorities.

UNDERSTAND THE SIGNS

Common Water Quality Problems

Learn about different signs of possible water-quality issues and what steps to take.

APPEARANCE

Unusual Water Colour

Water may appear yellow, brown, reddish or cloudy instead of clear.

Possible Causes

Rust, sediments, pipe corrosion or other suspended materials.

What Should You Do?

Avoid assuming the water is safe. Report the change and arrange appropriate water testing.

PHYSICAL IMPURITIES

Mud and Sediments

Visible mud, sand or particles may appear in stored or flowing water.

Possible Causes

Soil entering water sources, disturbed pipelines or tank deposits.

What Should You Do?

Avoid consuming visibly contaminated water and report the issue for inspection.

ODOUR

Bad or Unusual Smell

Water may have a rotten, musty, chemical or unusual smell.

Possible Causes

Organic matter, contamination, treatment chemicals or plumbing issues.

What Should You Do?

Do not drink water with an unusual unexplained smell until its safety has been checked.

TASTE

Unusual Water Taste

Water may have a salty, metallic, bitter or otherwise unusual taste.

Possible Causes

Dissolved minerals, pipe materials or possible contamination.

What Should You Do?

Stop drinking water with an unusual taste and arrange appropriate testing. Do not taste it again to investigate.

SAFETY CONCERN

Suspected Contamination

Water may be suspected of containing harmful microorganisms or chemicals.

Possible Causes

Sewage leakage, nearby waste, agricultural runoff or damaged pipelines.

What Should You Do?

Do not use it for drinking or cooking until authorities confirm a safe alternative or treatment method.

Note: Appearance, smell and taste alone cannot confirm whether water is safe or identify a specific contaminant. Appropriate laboratory testing is required.
